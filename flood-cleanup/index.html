Whether the water came from a storm, an overflowing river, a backed-up sewer, a failed sump pump or a burst plumbing line, flood damage needs a trained crew right away. Expect gray water (Category 2) or black water (Category 3) in almost any flood, and with it bacteria, contamination and potentially hazardous substances that threaten your health. Fountainebleau Restoration handles emergency flood cleanup across Fountainebleau and answers 24/7 to pump out the water, sanitize what it touched and restore your property safely.

Cleanup opens with rapid extraction: truck-mounted units and submersible pumps that pull thousands of gallons an hour out of the building. After that, IICRC-certified technicians on our team measure how contaminated the area is, then sanitize it with antimicrobial products, HEPA-filtered air cleaning and targeted disinfection of all surfaces the water reached. Anything unsalvageable comes out and is hauled off correctly, including wet insulation, drywall that took in contaminated water and waterlogged carpet padding.

Once the space is extracted and sanitized, our team brings in commercial drying gear, namely low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers plus high-velocity air movers, so the structural components dry all the way through. We check moisture levels every day with calibrated meters and thermal imaging so the drying is complete and mold does not get a start. Your insurance adjuster will want scope-of-work reports, equipment logs, moisture maps and photographs, so we keep that documentation going through the whole project.

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Make sure the ground slopes away from your foundation on every side
  • Keep documents and keepsakes in watertight bins on shelves, not on a basement floor
  • In areas of the property that tend to flood, raise electrical systems and appliances off the floor
  • Coat basement walls with waterproofing and seal any cracks in the foundation
  • Add a sump pump with battery backup, since power outages and storms tend to arrive together
  • Roof runoff should leave the downspout far enough out that it cannot soak the foundation

What a Professional Brings to Flood Cleanup

Flood cleanup is far more than pumping out standing water. Chemicals, bacteria, sewage and other contamination ride in with the flood and create real health dangers. A trained cleanup team knows how to set containment, wear PPE (personal protective equipment) correctly and sanitize by protocol, which protects your household from hepatitis, E. coli and other diseases carried in water.

Health is not the only issue: when cleanup is done wrong, moisture stays hidden and heavy mold can follow in a matter of days. Professional technicians use moisture meters, thermal imaging and psychrometric calculations to make sure every affected area, including wall cavities, subfloors and framing, reaches an acceptable drying standard. Drying by measurement is the one dependable method for avoiding secondary damage, which can add thousands to the repair bill.

How to Tell You Need Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Dirty water rising out of floor drains or sewer lines into rooms you use
  • Roof or wall openings left by a storm, with rain pouring through
  • Water collecting along the foundation because the yard is saturated
  • Basement flooding after the sump pump quit in a downpour
  • Appliances such as a water heater, washer or dishwasher spilling water across the floor
  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or drywall that feels soft and wet
