No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for Fountainebleau Restoration know it well. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • Every hour of run time matters, so resist switching the machines off for some quiet
  • Tell your restoration technician immediately if you notice new wet areas or odors during drying
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Make sure the technician can get in each day for monitoring visits

How to Tell You Need Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Trapped moisture often announces itself with a persistent musty odor after cleanup
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or blisters after a leak
  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running
  • Warped casing or baseboard where water reached the bottom of the wall
