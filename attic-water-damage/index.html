Attic Water Damage in Fountainebleau

Water reaches an attic from two directions. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. Whichever it is, few people ever go up and look. Our team in Fountainebleau inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

Warning Signs

  • After rain, a stain or brown ring on a top floor ceiling that looks worse
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • A musty smell in the attic or in the upstairs closets
  • Attic ducts and metal hangers showing drips, rust or water marks
  • Water dripping from, or staining around, a ceiling light or bathroom fan
  • Fuzzy white or black growth on the sheathing, usually thickest by the eaves
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Some bathroom fans exhaust into the attic and never reach the outdoors, adding warm, damp air daily. That moisture condenses on the cold sheathing and looks just like a roof leak.

Wet Wiring

An attic contains recessed light cans, junction boxes and runs of cable. Water in or on them is a shock and fire risk, so stay out until the power is checked.

Ceiling Collapse

Insulation full of water weighs a lot. Wet from above, drywall loses strength at its fasteners and may fall without warning, with the wet insulation coming down too.

Insulation That Stops Working

Once damp and packed down, insulation does little of its job and might not recover even when dry. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

How We Fix It

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Find the Source

We check the roof deck, flashing points, vents and exhaust ducts to learn whether the water is a leak, condensation, or both. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

We bag and remove insulation that is moldy or saturated, which lets the drywall and wood beneath it dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

3

Dry the Wood

Rafters, sheathing and joists are dried by air movers and dehumidifiers until their moisture levels are normal. An antimicrobial goes on after any visible mold has been cleaned from the wood.

4

Correct the Source

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. Once the attic stays dry, new insulation goes in to restore the R-value.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

The first storm

Insulation Takes It

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Can Start

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. The drywall may sag, and rings of brown staining appear on the ceiling of the room below.

Months later

Rot Sets In

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.
